# Source to Social Post

Turn source material into platform-native social copy without merely summarizing the article or repeating the previous post.

## Read the inputs

- Read the complete source material before selecting an angle. Use the appropriate source-specific skill or read tool for Feishu documents, webpages, or local files.
- Treat a supplied previous post as a de-duplication reference, not as evidence for new claims.
- Preserve the source's factual boundaries. Do not add unsupported product capabilities, statistics, or conclusions.

## Establish the narrative angle

Before drafting, state concisely:

1. The previous post's narrative angle, when a previous post is available.
2. The proposed new angle and the specific source ideas that support it.
3. The meaningful difference between the two angles.

Choose one central argument rather than recapping every section of the source. A strong new angle should change the reader's question, tension, or takeaway—not merely reorder the same examples.

When the user asks to discuss or agree on the framework first, present up to three genuinely distinct options, recommend one with a reason, and pause before drafting. Otherwise, give the brief angle comparison and continue directly to the drafts.

## Build the post

Use a compact structure appropriate to the chosen idea:

- Open with a clear tension, change, or counterintuitive claim.
- Explain why it matters in production or practice.
- Add only the source details needed to prove the point.
- End with a useful takeaway and a natural link CTA when a destination URL is available.

Do not turn a post into a catalog of every product or model mentioned in the source. Prefer synthesis over enumeration.

## Adapt by platform

### LinkedIn

- Write in concise, natural English unless the user requests another language.
- Use short paragraphs with a strong first two lines.
- Use bullets only for truly parallel, scannable criteria. Prefer one bullet block with three or four items at most; keep the rest as prose.
- Avoid decorative Unicode bold and excessive headings, emojis, or hashtags.
- Add two or three relevant hashtags when appropriate.

### X

- Default to one standalone post, not a thread.
- Keep it within X's post limit, allowing for the destination link. Favor one claim, one consequence, and one CTA.
- Do not compress the LinkedIn post mechanically; rewrite it around the same core angle.

## Deliverables

Unless the user specifies otherwise, provide separate `LinkedIn` and `X` sections. If the user requests a Markdown artifact, create or update a clearly named `.md` file containing:

- A short narrative-angle note
- The LinkedIn copy
- The X copy

When revising an existing artifact, change only the requested portion unless consistency requires a nearby edit.

## Final check

Confirm that:

- The new angle is traceable to the source.
- It does not duplicate the previous post's central narrative.
- The LinkedIn version reads as a post rather than an article outline.
- Bullets improve scanning instead of fragmenting the argument.
- The X version is self-contained and concise.
- Links, model names, and benchmark names match the source.
