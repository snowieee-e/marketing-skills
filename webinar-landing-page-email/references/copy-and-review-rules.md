# Landing Page Copy and Review Rules

## About the Webinar

Use a problem-and-response structure:

1. Describe a recognizable audience situation, tension, or cost.
2. Explain how the webinar helps the reader understand, evaluate, or act on it.

Avoid beginning with product history unless it is the event's main hook, assuming every prospect has one architecture, repeating every learning bullet, or claiming the session solves a problem it only explains.

## What You'll Learn

Preferred pattern:

> **Concise noun phrase** — Action verb + concrete learning outcome.

Give every bullet one job and one level of abstraction. Mention demos and AMAs as experiences. Add customer evidence only when the agenda includes it and permission is confirmed.

## Audience and positioning

- Identify the reader by their situation and decision, not only their title.
- Respect their current approach and acknowledge fair counterpoints when the sources do.
- Lead with the shared problem and introduce narrower personas only where relevant.

## Claims

Classify statements as confirmed facts, attributed points of view, roadmap, or uncertain. Omit or flag uncertain claims. Compare like with like and name the comparison dimension. Avoid unsupported words such as `better`, `more secure`, `easier`, or `guarantees`.

## Review checklist

1. Does the opening describe the primary persona's real problem?
2. Does the response explain how the webinar addresses it?
3. Does every bullet map to confirmed agenda content?
4. Are major agenda promises represented without repetition?
5. Are product names, relationships, and versions current?
6. Can every technical, customer, and competitive claim survive review?
7. Is the copy concise, parallel, and within the requested length?
8. Was a new numbered version created instead of overwriting history?
