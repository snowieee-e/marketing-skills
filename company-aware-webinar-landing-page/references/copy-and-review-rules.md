# Company-Aware Copy and Review Rules

## 1. Separate source authority

- Company context governs company description, product relationships, official terms, positioning, claims, and voice.
- Webinar sources govern session agenda, speakers, topics, examples, demos, and public promises.
- Never add a company-context claim to the learning agenda unless the webinar sources support it.
- Never use a convenient webinar phrase to override current official company naming.

## 2. About the Webinar

Use a problem-and-response structure:

1. Describe a recognizable audience situation or decision.
2. Explain how the webinar and relevant product capabilities help the reader understand or evaluate it.

Lead with audience relevance, not a generic company introduction. Use only the parts of company context that directly explain the webinar's value.

## 3. What You'll Learn

Use:

> **Concise noun phrase** — Action verb + concrete, agenda-supported takeaway.

Give each bullet one job. Keep bullets parallel, distinct, and at a consistent level of abstraction. Treat demos and AMAs as participant experiences.

## 4. Claims and competitive language

Classify statements as:

- Confirmed company fact
- Confirmed webinar content
- Company point of view
- Synthesized conclusion
- Roadmap
- Unconfirmed

Compare like with like and name the dimension: capability, architecture, deployment, ownership, operations, cost, performance, or another sourced dimension. Do not use `better`, `faster`, `easier`, `more secure`, or similar claims without evidence and scope.

## 5. Review checklist

Before saving a version, confirm:

1. Company context is current and based on official sources.
2. The opening describes the real decision audience.
3. The webinar response addresses the opening problem.
4. Every bullet maps to confirmed agenda content.
5. Official product names and relationships are correct.
6. Competitive language remains fair.
7. Customer names, metrics, roadmap items, and claims have approval.
8. Company context has not turned the page into a generic company advertisement.
9. The latest source revision is reflected.
10. A new numbered version was created instead of overwriting history.
