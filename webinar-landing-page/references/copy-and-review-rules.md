## 1. About the Webinar

Use a problem-and-response structure:

1. Describe a recognizable audience situation, tension, or cost.
2. Explain how the webinar will help the reader understand, evaluate, or act on it.

Avoid:

- Beginning with a product history unless it is the event's main hook
- Treating one common migration path as true for every prospect
- Ending with a detailed repetition of all learning bullets
- Claiming the webinar “solves” an operational problem when it only explains an option

## 2. What You'll Learn

Preferred pattern:

> **Concise noun phrase** — Action verb + concrete learning outcome.

Example:

> **Live Query Comparison** — See how the same retrieval pattern behaves across both systems and where each approach fits.

Rules:

- Give each bullet one job.
- Use a consistent level of abstraction.
- Do not mix broad outcomes with long component lists.
- Mention demos, workshops, or AMAs as experiences, not as passive media: prefer `Follow a live walkthrough` over `Watch a demo`.
- Do not add a case study merely because one exists in a whitepaper; the agenda must include it.
- If two bullets repeatedly need the same explanation, merge them or redraw the boundary.

## 3. Audience and positioning

- Identify the reader by their situation, not only their job title.
- Write to the decision they are making.
- Respect their current choice. Avoid implying their existing tool was irrational.
- Acknowledge where a competing or existing approach remains suitable when the source material does so.
- Lead with the problem shared by the broad target group. Introduce narrower personas in the relevant learning point.

## 4. Technical and competitive claims

Classify each statement before publishing:

- **Confirmed fact:** supported by official docs, approved deck, or cited evidence.
- **Company point of view:** attribute or frame as the case the speaker will make.
- **Roadmap:** do not promise availability or dates.
- **Draft/uncertain:** omit or flag for confirmation.

Use exact comparison dimensions such as:

- Capability
- Data or workload location
- Infrastructure ownership
- Operational responsibility
- Deployment model
- Licensing
- Cost or scale, when evidence supports it

Avoid vague constructions such as `better`, `more secure`, `easier`, or `guarantees privacy` without a stated dimension and source.

For competitive copy:

- Compare like with like.
- Preserve distinctions between products that have different licensing or architecture.
- Do not turn a company POV into a neutral industry fact.
- Include fair limitations or counterpoints when they are central to credibility.

## 5. Revision checklist

Before saving a version, ask:

1. Does the opening describe the primary persona's real problem?
2. Does the next paragraph clearly show how this webinar addresses it?
3. Does every bullet map to confirmed agenda content?
4. Does every major agenda promise appear somewhere in the page?
5. Are the bullets distinct, parallel, and ordered logically?
6. Are product names and relationships official and current?
7. Are claims precise enough to survive technical review?
8. Have repeated phrases and technical inventories been reduced?
9. Is the copy within the requested length?
10. Was a new numbered file created instead of overwriting history?
